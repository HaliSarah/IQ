01
The big shift

Stop asking. Start assigning.

Chat is question → answer: it tells you, then you do the work. An agent is goal → result: you hand it a goal, it plans, works and hands back a finished product. Keep chat for quick questions; send real work to an agent.

Chat (ask)"Write me ad copy." You still paste it into your email tool, design app and ads manager yourself.
Agent (assign)"Kick off the holiday campaign." It reads your brief, pulls product photos, drafts emails and ads, and leaves them ready for your approval.
02
Under the hood

Meet the Look–Decide–Do loop

Every agent runs the same loop until the job is done: look at the situation, decide the next step, do it, repeat. It stops when it reaches the finish line you described, so describe "done" clearly.

LookDecideDoLook again…Done ✓
Apps are just carsDifferent agent apps (workspaces) are different cars. Learn to drive once and you can drive any of them.
Define done"Find the top 10 podcasts and put them in a 10-slide deck" beats "research podcasts".
HelpersBig jobs may spin up helper copies of the agent to check things in parallel.
03
What you can tune

Turn the Four Dials

You don't build an agent from scratch. You tune one. A well-tuned budget model can beat a top model with no setup.

ContextWhat it should already know: you, your business, your voice.
ModelThe brain doing the thinking. Swap it any time; it changes often.
ToolsWhat it can touch: email, docs, browser, social, ads. (See Connector Atlas.)
SkillsYour step-by-step processes, saved for reuse. (See Skill Shelf.)
Treat setup like onboarding a brilliant new hire. Smart but brand new: it needs your background, your tools and your way of doing things.
04
Your agent's office

Build your HQ folder

Your agent lives in one folder on your computer that you own. Run everything from it, like a holding company with a sub-folder for each business or life pillar.

HQ/ # your whole operating system ├─ AGENTS.md # Home Base file: read every session ├─ context/ # who you are and what you sell ├─ about-me.md ├─ business.md ├─ offers.md ├─ ideal-customer.md ├─ brand-voice.md ├─ values.md ├─ lessons.md # corrections it learns ├─ skills/ # saved processes (see Skill Shelf) ├─ brand-one/ # a business pillar ├─ AGENTS.md # extra rules just for this pillar ├─ personal/ # health, family, goals ├─ active/ # one-off projects and experiments
Some apps keep settings in a hidden folder (it starts with a dot). On a Mac, press Cmd+Shift+. to show or hide them. Claude's own apps call the Home Base file CLAUDE.md; most others use AGENTS.md.
05
The one file that loads every time

Write your Home Base file

Your agent reads this file automatically at the start of every session. Keep it short and point it to everything else. Generate a draft with the Home Base builder at the bottom of this page.

HOME BASE STARTER# Who I am One paragraph about you and what you do. # My context: read before any task Everything about me and my business lives in /context. Load the relevant files before starting. Assumptions are the enemy: if the answer isn't there, ask me. # Lessons Check lessons.md before every task. When I correct you, add the rule there. # How I like to work Short, plain language. Real examples, never placeholders. Ask before sending, posting or spending.
06
Teach it your world

Fill your context library

Context files are company assets. Don't write them from scratch: have a chat interview you, then read every line before you save it. One wrong line ("our customers struggle with red skin") will leak into everything it writes.

about-meYour story, background, wins and turning points.
businessWhat you do, how you make money, your tools.
offersEvery product, price and how they link (upsells, bundles).
ideal-customerWho you serve, their pains, words they use.
brand-voiceTone, phrases you love, words you never use.
valuesWhat you stand for and won't do.
INTERVIEW PROMPTI want to build markdown context files for my AI agent: about-me, business, offers, ideal-customer, brand-voice and values. Interview me one question at a time to pull the information out of my head. After each section, show me the draft file so I can correct it. Keep facts objective and specific.
Talk, don't type. Voice dictation lets you answer at the speed you think.
07
Correct once, remembered forever

Start a Lessons Log

When you correct your agent ("always sign emails 'Cheers'", "dark mode for websites"), have it write the rule into lessons.md. You own the file, so it moves with you to any agent app.

SAY THIS WHEN YOU CORRECT ITAdd this to lessons.md as a rule so you never forget it: ___
Once a month: "Audit lessons.md and remove anything outdated, duplicated or contradictory."
08
Why agents get "dumb"

Keep the context lean

Each session has limited working memory. Endless chats fill it up until early details fall out and quality drops. Bloated context files eat that space before you even start.

New goal, new sessionStart fresh for each new task instead of one endless chat.
Dense, not longShort, high-value context files beat 100 rambling ones.
Tag what mattersPoint it at the exact folder or file (often with @) so it doesn't hunt.
09
Holding company thinking

Set global vs project rules

Things true everywhere (who you are, your values, how you work) live at the top of HQ. Things true for one business (its offers, its brand) live in that pillar's own Home Base file. They stack together.

Global (HQ)Your identity, values, lessons, universal skills like inbox triage.
Project (pillar)Brand-specific voice, offers, product-photo skill, client details.
10
Trust is earned

Set your safety rules

Connect tools with read-only access first. Raise permissions only as trust builds. Some actions should always need your yes.

Read-onlyAsk every timeAlways allow (low-risk only)
MoneyPayments, purchases and ad budgets: keep approval on.
PasswordsNever paste passwords or ID numbers into chat. If you connect a password manager, require fingerprint/approval.
SendingEmails, DMs and posts go to drafts first until you trust the output.
Sensitive dataKeep client, health and financial data out unless your plan and policy allow it.
Honest cost: a core AI plan plus a few tools is usually tens to a couple hundred dollars a month. Review subscriptions monthly; it's easy to forget trials.
11
Personal vs business

Know the two layers

Layer 1 is your personal agent workspace: an extension of you. Layer 2 is always-on business agents (customer support, reports) that run on a separate machine or cloud service, not your laptop. Same principles power both: context, tools and skills.

Layer 1 · PersonalYour HQ folder. You work inside it all day. Start here.
Layer 2 · BusinessShared, always-on agents for the team. Graduate here later.
12
If you have a team

Roll it out to your team

Outsiders can't learn every nuance of every job. Mix a light top-down audit with bottom-up training so each person automates their own role.

AuditAsk everyone: what tools do you use, what's repetitive, where are you stuck?
TrainTeach the HQ setup so each person builds their own context and skills.
CelebrateWeekly show-and-tell and a "skill of the month" prize make it culture.
Own the assetsDecide up front who owns shared skills and context files.
