How to Prepare for a Job Interview When You Don't Know What They'll Ask

The hardest part of interview prep isn't answering questions. It's guessing which questions are coming. Generic lists of "top 50 interview questions" don't help much when you're interviewing to be a nurse practitioner, a data engineer, or a special education teacher. Each field has its own technical questions, its own scenarios, and its own idea of a strong answer.

Prepare for the role, not just the interview

  • Study the questions your field actually asks. Hiring managers test for the skills the job uses every day.
  • Practice out loud. An answer that sounds good in your head often runs long when you say it.
  • Use real examples. A short story about a problem you solved beats a list of adjectives.
  • Bring your own questions. Asking about the team and the first 90 days shows you're already picturing the work.

How to Launch Your First Digital Product Without Guessing

Most people who want to sell a digital product get stuck in one of two places: they can't pick an idea, or they build something nobody was looking for. Both problems come from skipping the planning. A product that sells usually starts with the math and the market, not the design.

The launch path, in order

  • Run the numbers first. Decide what you want to earn, then figure out how many sales at what price it takes.
  • Find your sweet spot. Look where what you know, what you enjoy, and what people pay for overlap.
  • Pick the strongest idea and study competitors to see what buyers already like and what's missing.
  • Build fast. A focused first version you can make in a day beats a perfect one that never ships.
  • Sell with a clear page, an email that follows up, and content people can find later.

How to Set Up an AI Agent for Your Small Business (Safely)

An AI agent can research, draft, and schedule while you sleep. It can also post something off-brand, invent a statistic, or get access to accounts it never needed. The difference comes down to how you set it up. Think of it as hiring an assistant: give it a clear brief, only the keys it needs, and a rule that nothing goes public without your yes.

The basics of a safe setup

  • Write a one-page brief covering who you are, who you serve, how you sound, and what it must never do.
  • Give the least access possible. Use official sign-in buttons and never paste passwords or bank details into a chat.
  • Make it show its sources so you can check that the "demand" it finds is real.
  • Add an approval step. The agent drafts and schedules; you review before anything goes live.
  • Review weekly. Fifteen minutes to see what worked and fix what didn't keeps it improving.

"Get clients" is too big to act on. "Send five messages today" is not. The fastest way to your first paying clients is to start with a number you want to reach, work backward to the daily actions that get you there, and track every one.

Working backward from $1,000

  • Set the goal and do the math. How many clients at what price add up to $1,000?

  • Find one role model who already does what you want and learn from how they work.

  • Cut the plan in half when you fall behind, keeping only the actions that bring in money.

AI/ML Engineer Interview Questions: How to Prepare in 2026

AI/ML engineering interviews now cover two worlds at once: the classic machine learning fundamentals that haven't changed, and the fast-moving work of building with large language models. Strong candidates can explain why a model is failing, not just how to train one.

22%projected growth, 2025–35
Master'stypical entry education in that category

What interviewers test

  • ML fundamentals: overfitting, the bias-variance trade-off, and choosing the right evaluation metric for the problem.
  • Coding: usually Python, often working with data rather than pure algorithms.
  • ML system design: taking a model from raw data to production, then monitoring it for drift.
  • LLM work: when to prompt, when to fine-tune, how retrieval (RAG) helps, and how you evaluate output quality.

Words to know cold

Expect to hear terms like precision vs. recall, regularization, embeddings, data drift, RAG, hyperparameters. If any of them make you pause, practice until you can define each one in a sentence.
